- **⚠️ CLAIM FLAG — read before writing this one.** This is the highest-risk brief in the set and needs two guardrails. **(a)** Even my chosen primary contains "treatment" — I would publish it as `products for dark spots on face in Pakistan` or `brightening products for dark spots in Pakistan` and accept slightly lower keyword match in exchange for safety. **(b)** The adjacent high-volume cluster — `rang gora karne wali cream`, `best whitening cream in Pakistan`, `skin whitening serum`, `gora rang`, `which vitamin c cream is best for skin whitening` — is **deliberately excluded**. Those queries demand a shade-change promise. The SERP for them is hydroquinone and tretinoin products (Lazma 30g around Rs 220–485, Skinoren, Sanclear) at Rs 200–900 and fairness creams; several are prescription-only. You cannot serve that intent without a claim, you cannot compete on that price, and you should not try. Similarly `melasma ka ilaj` / `melasma treatment` is excluded — derma.pk ranks there with compounded prescription serums (5% tranexamic + 4% niacinamide at Rs 2,150, marked dispensing medicine, prescription-only) that you do not stock. **Target the concern, never the outcome.** Your collection is named `/collections/brightening`, which is already the right frame — stay inside it.
- **Intent:** Commercial investigation on Pakistan's single most emotionally loaded skin concern.
- **What ranking pages appear to cover:** split in two. Medical-ish pages (derma.pk *"Melasma, Freckles & Dark Spots: A Treatment Guide for Pakistan"*, oladoc, instacare) cover hydroquinone, tretinoin and in-clinic options, with derma.pk noting gradual change over 8–12 weeks with daily sunscreen and that melasma can be controlled but not always permanently resolved. Commercial pages (markaz.app, olim.pk, skinfactor.pk, hairenergyofficial.com, iglow.pk) sell kojic acid, alpha arbutin and niacinamide serums at Rs 630–2,450, in heavy whitening language.
- **The gap you fill:** there is a wide-open middle. Nobody writes the cosmetic, honest, non-whitening page for Pakistani readers — one that distinguishes the *types* of marks, because the right product differs completely: post-acne marks (which often fade on their own and mainly need you to stop picking plus daily SPF), sun-driven pigmentation, and melasma (which is hormonal, stubborn and a dermatologist's job — say so and refer out, which builds more trust than overselling). Then the point every commercial page buries: **without daily sunscreen nothing in this category does much**, because UV drives the pigment. That single honest statement is both correct and the best possible argument for the SPF collection you also sell. Set realistic timelines and say plainly what these products do not do.
